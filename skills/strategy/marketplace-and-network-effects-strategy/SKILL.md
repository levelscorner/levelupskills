# Marketplace and Network Effects Strategy

## Overview
Use this skill to think through marketplaces and network-effect businesses where the product is shaped by interactions between participants. The key question is whether the business can create enough trust and liquidity for those interactions to compound.

## When to Use
Use this skill when the main problem is **multi-sided market design**.

Typical triggers:
- supply-demand balancing
- cold-start wedge design
- liquidity constraints
- trust and safety in participant interaction
- network-effect defensibility questions
- take-rate and marketplace monetization logic

Do **not** use this as the main skill when:
- the business is basically single-product SaaS with no meaningful cross-side dynamics
- the issue is general growth experimentation rather than market-structure strategy → use `gtm-and-growth-experiments`
- the issue is one specific partnership rather than ecosystem market design → use `partnerships-and-business-development`

## Core Principle
A marketplace wins when participant incentives, liquidity, trust, and transaction mechanics reinforce each other faster than coordination costs grow.

## Always cover
- sides of market
- unit of liquidity
- trust and safety needs
- cold-start wedge
- monetization and take-rate logic
- defensibility risks

## Workflow
1. Identify the sides of the market and the core transaction or interaction.
2. Diagnose the cold-start problem, trust bottlenecks, and liquidity constraints.
3. Evaluate incentives, concentration risks, and disintermediation threats.
4. Recommend sequencing for supply, demand, trust, and monetization.
5. Separate apparent network effects from genuine compounding defensibility.

## Output format
Return:
1. marketplace diagnosis
2. recommended launch / growth sequence
3. network-effect logic
4. risks and failure modes
5. priority experiments

## Common pitfalls
1. **Calling any user base a network effect.** Interaction structure matters more than buzzwords.
2. **Trying to scale both sides at once with no wedge.** Cold start usually needs asymmetric focus.
3. **Ignoring trust and safety costs.** Unsafe interactions can kill liquidity fast.
4. **Confusing volume with liquidity.** Lots of users can still mean poor matching quality.
5. **Assuming defensibility arrives automatically.** Many marketplaces stay fragile for longer than founders expect.

## Verification checklist
- [ ] I identified the actual sides and unit of liquidity.
- [ ] I separated cold-start strategy from long-run defensibility.
- [ ] I named trust, safety, or disintermediation risks.
- [ ] I recommended priority experiments instead of vague marketplace theory.

## Examples
### Example: early marketplace diagnosis
Return:
1. Supply concentration is the main bottleneck, not demand generation.
2. Start with one narrow wedge geography or workflow.
3. Build trust mechanics before adding broad expansion loops.
4. Measure successful matches and repeat participation, not just signups.
5. Revisit take-rate only after reliable transaction flow exists.
