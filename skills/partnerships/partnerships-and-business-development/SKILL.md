# Partnerships and Business Development

## Overview
Use this skill to design and evaluate partnerships as a real company function, not as random opportunistic conversations. The key question is whether a relationship can create repeatable value through aligned incentives and workable execution.

## When to Use
Use this skill when the main problem is **partnership strategy or structure**.

Typical triggers:
- channel or distribution partnership design
- integration partnership evaluation
- reseller, referral, co-sell, or ecosystem strategy
- partnership economics and owner model
- deciding whether a partnership is tactical noise or strategic leverage

Do **not** use this as the main skill when:
- the task is direct pipeline or discovery execution → use `founder-sales-for-b2b` or `b2b-sales-discovery-and-pipeline`
- the task is broad channel experimentation without a partnership structure question → use `gtm-and-growth-experiments`
- the task is mostly messaging polish for a partner page

## Core Principle
A partnership is only valuable when incentives, distribution mechanics, and ongoing ownership are clear enough that both sides can repeatedly act.

## Always cover
- partner type and strategic fit
- incentive alignment
- operational complexity
- revenue and distribution logic
- ownership and review cadence
- failure modes

## Workflow
1. Clarify the product, ICP, and why a partnership is being considered.
2. Identify the partnership type: channel, integration, referral, co-sell, reseller, ecosystem, or co-marketing.
3. Evaluate mutual incentive alignment, execution complexity, and expected upside.
4. Recommend structure, sequencing, owner behavior, and success metrics.
5. Distinguish near-term deal value from long-term strategic value.

## Output format
Return:
1. partnership context diagnosis
2. recommended partnership model
3. execution and ownership plan
4. risks and misalignment points
5. success metrics and next steps

## Common pitfalls
1. **Calling introductions a strategy.** One-off access is not a partnership system.
2. **Ignoring owner workload.** Many partnerships die because nobody truly runs them.
3. **Overestimating strategic upside without distribution proof.** Brand adjacency alone is weak.
4. **Mismatching incentives.** If one side wins only on paper, the motion will stall.
5. **Treating co-marketing as distribution.** Awareness is not the same as pipeline.

## Verification checklist
- [ ] I identified the actual partnership type.
- [ ] I assessed incentives, execution burden, and owner model.
- [ ] I separated tactical upside from durable strategic leverage.
- [ ] I defined success metrics or review triggers.

## Examples
### Example: integration partnership
Return:
1. The real value is workflow distribution, not logo prestige.
2. Recommend a lightweight integration first, not a large alliance agreement.
3. Assign one owner for partner success and one for technical upkeep.
4. Measure activated shared customers, not announcement reach.
5. Reassess after the first proof of pull-through demand.
