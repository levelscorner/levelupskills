# Startup Finance and Unit Economics

## Overview
Use this skill to translate startup activity into economic reality: how money moves, what actually drives margin, and which decisions improve or damage the business model. The goal is not spreadsheet theater. The goal is financial truth good enough to guide action.

## When to Use
Use this skill when the main problem is **economic clarity and operating-finance judgment**.

Typical triggers:
- runway or burn anxiety
- weak understanding of cost drivers
- confusion between revenue growth and healthy economics
- budgeting or hiring decisions under cash constraint
- scenario planning for growth, pricing, or spend changes
- unclear payback or margin dynamics

Do **not** use this as the main skill when:
- the main issue is pricing-plan design → use `pricing-and-packaging-strategy`
- the task is compliance, tax filing, or jurisdiction-specific legal/accounting advice
- the question is only payment-provider selection → use `payment-gateway-selection-for-software`

## Core Principle
Finance is not bookkeeping alone. Finance is the discipline that reveals whether growth creates value or only creates more expense.

## Always cover
- burn and runway
- gross / contribution margin
- pricing logic
- cost drivers
- reporting cadence
- best / base / worst case scenarios

## Workflow
1. Identify revenue model and cost structure.
2. Separate fixed, variable, and semi-variable costs.
3. Estimate runway, burn, and cash sensitivity.
4. Evaluate pricing, margin, and payback implications.
5. Recommend operator-level actions, not only accounting observations.

## Output format
Return:
1. current financial model summary
2. major risks
3. unit-economics view
4. recommended actions
5. metrics to track now

## Common pitfalls
1. **Revenue obsession without margin understanding.** Top line alone can hide a bad business.
2. **Ignoring payment, infra, support, and tax costs.** These often erode margins quietly.
3. **Pricing decisions without willingness-to-pay or cost context.** That creates distorted signals.
4. **No scenario planning.** Teams then mistake one forecast for certainty.
5. **Mixing cash, profit, and runway language carelessly.** That confuses decisions.

## Verification checklist
- [ ] I separated cash, revenue, and margin issues.
- [ ] I identified the real cost drivers.
- [ ] I included scenario thinking where uncertainty matters.
- [ ] I gave actionable operator recommendations, not just observations.

## Examples
### Example: runway pressure
Return:
1. The company has more growth ambition than cash tolerance.
2. Separate mandatory spend from speculative growth spend.
3. Model best, base, and worst cases around revenue timing and hiring.
4. Protect gross-margin understanding before cutting blindly.
5. Turn finance review into a repeatable founder ritual.

### Example: weak unit economics
Return:
1. Revenue exists, but contribution margin is being flattered by ignored support and infra costs.
2. Add customer-level or segment-level cost visibility.
3. Reassess pricing and servicing model together.
4. Slow scale on low-quality revenue if payback is weak.
5. Track payback, margin, and cash effect as linked metrics.
