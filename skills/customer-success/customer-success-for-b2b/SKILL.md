# Customer Success for B2B

## Overview
Use this skill to design customer-success motions that improve onboarding, adoption, retention, and expansion in B2B contexts. The goal is not to check in politely. The goal is to make sure the buyer reaches the promised outcome in a way that sustains trust and commercial durability.

## When to Use
Use this skill when the main problem is **post-sale account success**.

Typical triggers:
- onboarding is weak or inconsistent
- activation is slow
- stakeholders are misaligned after purchase
- renewals feel risky or unpredictable
- expansion opportunities are invisible
- account health is being judged informally rather than systematically

Do **not** use this as the main skill when:
- the issue is reactive support queue design → use `customer-support-and-feedback-ops`
- the issue is pre-sale discovery or pipeline movement → use `founder-sales-for-b2b` or `b2b-sales-discovery-and-pipeline`
- the company does not yet have meaningful post-sale customer behavior to manage

## Core Principle
Customer success is not reactive support. It is the discipline of making sure the buyer gets the promised outcome in a way that sustains trust, retention, and expansion.

## Always cover
- onboarding path
- time-to-value
- stakeholder mapping
- health signals and risk flags
- renewal / expansion readiness
- feedback loop into product

## Workflow
1. Clarify product type, account profile, stakeholders, and lifecycle stage.
2. Diagnose whether the problem is onboarding, adoption, ROI visibility, renewal risk, stakeholder misalignment, or internal ownership.
3. Recommend a success motion sized to company stage and account economics.
4. Define account health signals, intervention triggers, and communication rhythm.
5. Convert repeated account friction into product and process learning.

## Output format
Return:
1. success-motion diagnosis
2. recommended lifecycle playbook
3. health metrics and triggers
4. risk accounts / failure modes
5. next actions and system lessons

## Common pitfalls
1. **Treating customer success as polite follow-up.** It should drive outcomes, not just touchpoints.
2. **No time-to-value definition.** Customers cannot succeed against a vague promise.
3. **Relying on relationship sentiment alone.** Health needs behavioral evidence.
4. **Separating success too far from product feedback.** Repeated friction should change the product or process.
5. **Pushing expansion before value is proven.** That damages trust fast.

## Verification checklist
- [ ] I identified the post-sale lifecycle problem clearly.
- [ ] I defined time-to-value, health signals, or renewal-risk logic.
- [ ] I separated success work from support or sales work.
- [ ] I tied the motion to retention, trust, and expansion outcomes.

## Examples
### Example: onboarding problem
Return:
1. The real issue is slow activation, not weak relationship management.
2. Create a structured first-30-days onboarding path with one clear success milestone.
3. Track time-to-value, admin setup completion, and first recurring use.
4. Escalate accounts where a champion exists but cross-functional adoption is absent.
5. Feed repeated setup friction back into product onboarding.

### Example: renewal risk
Return:
1. The account is using the product, but ROI is not visible to decision-makers.
2. Add a stakeholder review cadence before renewal season.
3. Build lightweight success proof around usage, outcomes, and next opportunities.
4. Define early warning signals instead of discovering risk at renewal time.
5. Hold expansion until core value is clear and trusted.
