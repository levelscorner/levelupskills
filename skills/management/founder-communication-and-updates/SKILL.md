# Founder Communication and Updates

## Overview
Use this skill to structure founder communication so updates are clear, useful, and trust-building across stakeholders. The point is not to sound polished. The point is to reduce ambiguity while keeping confidence, candor, and direction intact.

## When to Use
Use this skill when the task is about **stakeholder communication from a founder or operator seat**.

Typical triggers:
- investor update
- founder note to team
- board-style summary
- stakeholder status message
- update under uncertainty, delay, or risk
- alignment message where the audience needs signal, not spin

Do **not** use this as the main skill when:
- the task is only generic formatting or brevity → use the communication skills
- the task is brand copy, positioning, or external marketing narrative → use `brand-positioning-and-messaging`
- the task is internal planning rather than outward communication → use `founder-operating-system`

## Core Principle
Strong founder communication reduces ambiguity without pretending uncertainty does not exist.

## Always cover
- audience and stakes
- what changed
- what matters now
- risks, unknowns, or blockers
- decisions, asks, or next steps
- tone that preserves trust

## Workflow
1. Identify audience, purpose, and decision context.
2. Determine what must be communicated: progress, blockers, risks, asks, decisions, or narrative framing.
3. Choose the right level of detail and candor for that audience.
4. Emphasize signal over noise.
5. Make next steps, ownership, and asks unmistakable.

## Output format
Return:
1. communication context
2. recommended structure
3. draft outline or message logic
4. risk / tone guidance
5. follow-up actions

## Common pitfalls
1. **Performing confidence instead of communicating reality.** Stakeholders can smell spin.
2. **Dumping raw detail without hierarchy.** An update should help people orient quickly.
3. **Hiding the ask.** If action is needed, say so clearly.
4. **Confusing optimism with omission.** Unknowns and risks should be named when they matter.
5. **Using the same update style for every audience.** Investors, team members, and partners need different emphasis.

## Verification checklist
- [ ] I identified the audience and their decision needs.
- [ ] I separated signal from detail noise.
- [ ] I named risks or uncertainty where they matter.
- [ ] I made the ask, decision, or next step unmistakable.

## Examples
### Example: investor update
Return structure:
1. What moved this month
2. What is working
3. What is off-plan
4. Main risk
5. Specific ask or next milestone

### Example: team update after delay
Return structure:
1. What slipped
2. Why it slipped
3. What changes now
4. What remains stable
5. Owner and next checkpoint
