# Hiring and People Ops for Startups

## Overview
Use this skill to design early hiring and people-ops decisions that improve leverage without creating premature organizational complexity. The goal is not to hire quickly at any cost. The goal is to add capability where it unlocks the company most.

## When to Use
Use this skill when the main problem is **who to hire, when to hire, and how to support them sanely**.

Typical triggers:
- founder is unsure what role to hire next
- role definitions are muddy
- contractor vs employee decisions are unclear
- onboarding is ad hoc
- people-ops basics are missing
- headcount is growing faster than role clarity

Do **not** use this as the main skill when:
- the issue is org structure after the first hires → use `org-design-after-first-hires`
- the issue is company-wide operating cadence → use `founder-operating-system`
- the task is writing a job ad with no broader hiring-system question

## Core Principle
Early hiring should reduce real bottlenecks, not merely make the company feel bigger or safer.

## Always cover
- current bottleneck
- role need and ownership logic
- contractor vs employee trade-off
- onboarding basics
- compensation / cost realism
- next likely hiring trigger

## Workflow
1. Identify the company's current bottleneck and what work is not getting done well.
2. Clarify the job to be done before naming the role.
3. Evaluate whether the need is permanent, part-time, contractor, or founder-owned for longer.
4. Recommend a role definition, hiring sequence, and minimal onboarding system.
5. State the trigger that would justify the next hire.

## Output format
Return:
1. hiring-need diagnosis
2. recommended role / hiring sequence
3. contractor vs employee view
4. onboarding and people-ops basics
5. cost, risk, and next trigger

## Common pitfalls
1. **Hiring to reduce founder anxiety.** The role should solve a real bottleneck.
2. **Naming a title before defining the work.** This creates role confusion fast.
3. **Using full-time hires where lighter options would work.** Fixed cost can outrun certainty.
4. **Ignoring onboarding.** A good hire can still fail in a messy system.
5. **Hiring ahead of role clarity.** More people can amplify ambiguity.

## Verification checklist
- [ ] I identified the real bottleneck first.
- [ ] I defined the work before the title.
- [ ] I assessed contractor vs employee trade-offs.
- [ ] I included onboarding and next-hire trigger logic.

## Examples
### Example: first non-founder hire
Return:
1. The company needs relief on one repeated execution bottleneck, not a prestige hire.
2. Define the outcome the hire must own before choosing the title.
3. Consider contract or part-time support if certainty is low.
4. Build a simple onboarding path before the person starts.
5. Reassess after the bottleneck is genuinely reduced.

### Example: chaotic hiring plan
Return:
1. Current issue is role confusion, not talent scarcity alone.
2. Sequence hires around company bottlenecks, not aspirational org charts.
3. Tighten job scope and ownership boundaries.
4. Add simple people-ops hygiene early.
5. Delay extra headcount until the current structure can absorb it.
