# Customer Research and User Interviews

## Overview
Use this skill to turn user conversations into product learning rather than confirmation theater. The goal is not to ask nice questions. The goal is to uncover real behavior, pain, context, and decision logic.

## When to Use
Use this skill when the main problem is **how to learn from users qualitatively**.

Typical triggers:
- user interviews feel shallow or biased
- the team is validating assumptions with anecdotes
- product decisions need deeper context from real users
- feedback is plentiful but not turning into insight
- the team needs a better research approach before roadmap changes

Do **not** use this as the main skill when:
- the issue is quantitative measurement design → use `analytics-and-metrics-system`
- the issue is deciding what to build from a broader prioritization lens → use `product-discovery-and-prioritization`
- the conversation is actually a sales call rather than user-learning work

## Core Principle
Good customer research captures lived behavior, constraints, and goals more reliably than stated preferences or flattering opinions.

## Always cover
- research objective
- target participant logic
- interview structure
- bias and leading-question risk
- synthesis approach
- decision use of findings

## Workflow
1. Identify the decision the research is supposed to inform.
2. Clarify which participants matter and why.
3. Recommend interview structure focused on behavior, context, and pain.
4. Define how findings will be synthesized and compared.
5. Convert insights into product decisions or next research questions.

## Output format
Return:
1. research objective diagnosis
2. recommended interview plan
3. question and bias guidance
4. synthesis method
5. decision implications and next research steps

## Common pitfalls
1. **Asking users what features they want and treating that as truth.** Stated preference is weak evidence.
2. **Running interviews without a decision question.** That creates interesting but useless notes.
3. **Leading participants toward the answer the team wants.** Confirmation is not research.
4. **Collecting quotes without synthesis.** Raw anecdotes do not make a strategy.
5. **Confusing support complaints with whole-market insight.** Source context matters.

## Verification checklist
- [ ] I defined the decision the research should inform.
- [ ] I matched the participant set to that decision.
- [ ] I recommended behavior-focused interview structure.
- [ ] I included synthesis and decision-use logic.

## Examples
### Example: weak interview program
Return:
1. The problem is not lack of interviews; it is lack of research discipline.
2. Anchor the work on one product decision.
3. Use questions about past behavior and current workflow, not hypotheticals first.
4. Synthesize across patterns instead of collecting isolated quotes.
5. Feed the insights into explicit product choices.

### Example: founder hearing mixed feedback
Return:
1. The issue is unclear segmentation of who is speaking and why.
2. Separate different user types before interpreting the feedback.
3. Re-run interviews with tighter objective and structure.
4. Compare complaints against observed behavior and product usage.
5. Do not change roadmap on emotional recency alone.
