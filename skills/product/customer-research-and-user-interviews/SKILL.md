# Customer Research and User Interviews

## Overview

Use this skill to design and interpret customer conversations so they produce product learning instead of polite noise.

## Core Principle

Customer interviews are useful when they reveal behavior, workflow, pain, and willingness-to-change — not when they merely collect opinions about hypothetical features.

## Workflow

1. Clarify the learning goal: problem discovery, workflow mapping, pricing signal, reaction to prototype, churn diagnosis, or message testing.
2. Define who should be interviewed and why.
3. Recommend an interview structure that prioritizes past behavior over speculative preference.
4. Synthesize patterns, contradictions, and next decisions.
5. Turn insights into product, GTM, or research next steps.

## Always cover

- research objective
- target interview profile
- question design
- synthesis method
- confidence / sample limitations
- decision implications

## Output format

Return:
1. research objective
2. interview plan or interview diagnosis
3. key questions / structure
4. synthesis and insights
5. recommended next actions

## Common pitfalls

- asking leading questions
- over-valuing compliments and weak enthusiasm
- treating small samples as certainty
- jumping from anecdotes directly into roadmap commitments

## Verification checklist

- [ ] I centered the research objective.
- [ ] I emphasized behavior over hypotheticals.
- [ ] I separated signals from politeness.
- [ ] I turned findings into decision-ready next steps.
