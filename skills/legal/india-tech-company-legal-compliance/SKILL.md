# India Tech Company Legal Compliance

## Overview
Use this skill to frame India-specific legal and compliance groundwork for technology companies. The goal is not to imitate a law firm memo. The goal is to help a founder or operator understand what categories matter, what can be staged, and where real counsel is needed.

## When to Use
Use this skill when the main problem is **India-specific company legal/compliance readiness**.

Typical triggers:
- deciding what compliance groundwork an Indian startup needs
- understanding company, contract, privacy, labor, or payments-adjacent legal basics
- preparing for customers, investors, vendors, or hiring with better legal hygiene
- separating must-do-now from later formalization

Do **not** use this as the main skill when:
- the company or governing jurisdiction is not India
- the task requires definitive legal advice, drafting, or filing decisions that need counsel
- the real issue is security/privacy control design rather than India legal scope → use `security-privacy-and-risk-review`

## Important framing
This skill is for structured guidance, not a substitute for qualified Indian legal, tax, CS, CA, labor, or regulatory advice.

## Core Principle
Legal readiness is not about collecting every document early. It is about reducing avoidable company-risk, contract-risk, and compliance-risk in a sequence the business can actually sustain.

## Default scope
- company and governance basics
- founder paper hygiene
- customer and vendor contracts
- privacy / terms / policy readiness
- employment / contractor basics
- payments / invoicing / statutory awareness
- escalation points for real counsel

## Workflow
1. Identify stage, entity situation, business model, and exposure surface.
2. Separate immediate must-haves from later governance maturity.
3. Map the legal/compliance areas touched by product, hiring, payments, and customer contracts.
4. Flag where specialist review is required.
5. Recommend an order of operations rather than an undifferentiated checklist.

## Output format
Return:
1. India-specific legal readiness diagnosis
2. must-have-now items
3. later-stage compliance and governance items
4. key contract / privacy / people risks
5. items requiring qualified counsel or specialist review

## Common pitfalls
1. **Treating legal work as purely administrative.** It shapes risk and trust.
2. **Ignoring founder, IP, or contract hygiene until too late.** Cleanup later is expensive.
3. **Using copied templates without fit.** Context matters.
4. **Confusing staged compliance with no compliance.** “Later” still needs logic.
5. **Assuming internet folklore is legal advice.** It is not.

## Verification checklist
- [ ] I kept the scope India-specific.
- [ ] I separated operational guidance from actual legal advice.
- [ ] I identified immediate versus later-stage needs.
- [ ] I called out where qualified counsel or specialists are required.

## Examples
### Example: early SaaS company
Return:
1. Main issue is basic company, founder, contract, and privacy hygiene.
2. Prioritize core paper trail, customer contract readiness, and policy basics.
3. Do not overbuild governance before real exposure exists.
4. Escalate IP, employment, and privacy issues where facts are sensitive.
5. Keep a staged compliance tracker instead of scattered notes.

### Example: enterprise buyer pressure
Return:
1. The buyer is creating trust pressure before the company has mature legal operations.
2. Tighten contracts, privacy posture, and company paper readiness first.
3. Coordinate with security/privacy review so the legal story matches operational reality.
4. Avoid promising compliance positions you cannot support.
5. Bring in counsel for negotiated contract or regulatory questions.
