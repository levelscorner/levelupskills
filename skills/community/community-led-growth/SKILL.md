# Community-Led Growth

## Overview
Use this skill to think about community as an intentional company function tied to retention, advocacy, contribution, and product momentum. The goal is not noisy engagement. The goal is compounding participation that improves company outcomes.

## When to Use
Use this skill when the main problem is **community design**, not just broadcasting.

Typical triggers:
- building a member community around a product
- designing participation loops or contribution systems
- improving onboarding, advocacy, or member retention
- deciding whether community should support acquisition, activation, retention, or learning

Do **not** use this as the main skill when:
- the task is mostly content distribution or audience acquisition → use `b2c-growth-and-content-loops`
- the task is mostly brand narrative or message clarity → use `brand-positioning-and-messaging`
- the product does not benefit from repeated participant interaction yet

## Core Principle
Community works when members gain identity, utility, and meaningful participation rather than being treated as passive followers.

## Always cover
- audience and purpose
- community model
- loop design and incentives
- moderation / stewardship needs
- retention and advocacy signals
- failure modes

## Workflow
1. Clarify audience, product relationship, and desired community role.
2. Diagnose whether the need is acquisition, activation, retention, contribution, or advocacy.
3. Recommend the right community model and loop structure.
4. Define participation mechanics, norms, and stewardship.
5. Distinguish vanity engagement from compounding community value.

## Output format
Return:
1. community-growth diagnosis
2. recommended model
3. loop and participation design
4. risks and moderation needs
5. metrics and next experiments

## Common pitfalls
1. **Mistaking audience size for community strength.** Followers are not the same as members.
2. **Launching a community with no clear member job.** People do not stay for abstraction.
3. **Ignoring stewardship.** A community without norms and care often decays fast.
4. **Chasing engagement metrics with no product linkage.** The loop should matter to the business.
5. **Trying to scale before the first useful interactions are real.** Start with value, not volume.

## Verification checklist
- [ ] I identified the actual purpose of the community.
- [ ] I tied the design to acquisition, activation, retention, contribution, or advocacy.
- [ ] I separated vanity engagement from durable value.
- [ ] I named moderation or stewardship needs.

## Examples
### Example: early-stage product community
Return:
1. Goal is retention and user learning, not mass acquisition.
2. Start with a small practitioner community, not a broad public forum.
3. Create repeatable prompts for members to share wins, failures, and workflows.
4. Founder or operator should actively steward the first cohort.
5. Measure return participation and useful product feedback, not likes alone.
